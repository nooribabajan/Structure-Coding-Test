STEP 01 — HEALTH

Bacterial Diseases in Humans — Overview

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Pathogenic bacteria can cause disease through invasion, toxin production, tissue damage, or harmful host responses. For each disease, connect the causative bacterium with transmission, major features, treatment principle, and prevention. Core curriculum examples include cholera, typhoid, tuberculosis, and pneumonia; textbook coverage also includes bacillary dysentery and tetanus.
Learning Check
Why should a bacterial disease be learned with its causative organism and transmission route?
STEP 02 — HEALTH

Cholera — Vibrio cholerae

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Cholera is an acute intestinal disease caused by Vibrio cholerae. It is commonly associated with contaminated water or food. Cholera toxin disturbs intestinal ion and water balance, producing profuse watery diarrhoea and potentially rapid dehydration. Prevention depends on safe water, sanitation, hygiene, and food safety; treatment requires prompt rehydration and appropriate clinical care.
Learning Check
Connect contaminated water → intestinal infection → fluid loss → dehydration.
STEP 03 — HEALTH

Typhoid — Salmonella Typhi

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella enterica serovar Typhi and is mainly transmitted by the fecal–oral route through contaminated food or water. It can cause prolonged fever and systemic illness. Prevention relies on sanitation, safe water, food hygiene, and vaccination where recommended; treatment requires appropriate antibiotics under clinical guidance.
Learning Check
What transmission route links contaminated food/water to typhoid?
STEP 04 — HEALTH

Tuberculosis — Mycobacterium tuberculosis

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Tuberculosis is caused primarily by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and most often affects the lungs. It spreads through airborne particles released by an infected person. Persistent cough, fever, night sweats, weight loss, and fatigue are important clinical clues. Control requires early diagnosis, appropriate multidrug treatment, and measures that reduce transmission.
Learning Check
Why must TB control address both the infected person and transmission?
STEP 05 — HEALTH

Pneumonia — Bacterial Infection of the Lungs

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Bacterial pneumonia is an infection and inflammation of lung tissue. Multiple bacterial species can cause it. Inflammation and fluid or cellular material in alveoli can interfere with gas exchange, causing cough, fever, chest discomfort, and breathing difficulty. Diagnosis and treatment depend on the clinical setting and causative organism.
Learning Check
How can alveolar inflammation interfere with gas exchange?
STEP 06 — HEALTH

Important Bacterial Diseases of Plants

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Plant-pathogenic bacteria can produce disease patterns including leaf spots, blights, soft rots, wilts, and galls. These diseases can reduce crop quality and yield. Management includes sanitation, removal of infected material, clean planting material, resistant varieties where available, and appropriate agricultural disease-management practices.
Learning Check
Why are bacterial plant diseases important to agriculture?
STEP 07 — HEALTH

Plant Disease Patterns — Spot, Blight, Soft Rot, Wilt & Gall

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Leaf spots are localized lesions; blights involve rapid and extensive tissue death; soft rots cause watery tissue breakdown; wilts involve loss of turgor often linked with disturbed water transport; galls are abnormal localized swellings or growths. Symptoms should be interpreted with host and causative organism rather than used alone for diagnosis.
Learning Check
Which pattern is defined by abnormal localized swelling?
STEP 08 — HEALTH

Bacterial Flora of Humans — Normal Microbiota

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Normal human microbiota are microorganisms that ordinarily inhabit particular body sites without necessarily causing disease. Important sites include skin, mouth, gastrointestinal tract, and other mucosal surfaces. Resident bacteria can contribute to ecosystem stability and help prevent pathogen colonization.
Learning Check
Why is normal flora not synonymous with pathogens?
STEP 09 — HEALTH

Benefits of Human Bacterial Flora

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Normal microbiota provide colonization resistance by competing with pathogens for nutrients and attachment sites and by producing inhibitory substances. They can also contribute to nutrient processing and production of some vitamins. The host–microbe relationship is dynamic and can change when the microbial community is disturbed.
Learning Check
Give two mechanisms by which normal microbiota protect the host.
STEP 10 — CONTROL

Physical Control — Heat & Pasteurization

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Physical control methods reduce microbial populations using physical conditions such as heat. Pasteurization is a controlled heat treatment designed to reduce harmful or spoilage microorganisms in foods and liquids; it is not equivalent to sterilization. Treatment conditions depend on the product and validated process.
Learning Check
Why is pasteurization not the same as sterilization?
STEP 11 — CONTROL

Sterilization, Disinfection & Antisepsis

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Sterilization aims to eliminate all viable microorganisms, including resistant forms such as endospores. Disinfection is applied to non-living surfaces to destroy or reduce microorganisms to an appropriate level. Antisepsis uses suitable antimicrobial chemicals on living tissue. Correct selection depends on the site, material, target organisms, and required level of control.
Learning Check
Match the correct method to skin, a non-living surface, and a material that must be sterile.
STEP 12 — CONTROL

Chemical Antimicrobial Agents & Responsible Control

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Chemical control includes disinfectants and antiseptics, while therapeutic antimicrobial agents are used to treat infections. Control should be targeted: the agent, concentration, contact conditions, site, and required level of microbial reduction matter. Antibiotic use should be clinically appropriate; antimicrobial resistance makes unnecessary or incorrect use a serious public-health concern.
Learning Check
Why must an antimicrobial agent be matched to its application site and purpose?
STEP 13 — PRACTICAL

Practical Work — Safe Bacterial Observation & Identification

Topic-linked visual
Teaching Material Use prepared slides, high-quality images, or models to identify cocci, bacilli, and spiral forms and interpret Gram-staining outcomes. Compare Gram-positive and Gram-negative organization and relate observations to cell-wall structure. Safe practical work uses prepared material, models, or image interpretation rather than culturing pathogenic bacteria.
Learning Check
Identify the observation first, then explain the biological reason for the observed pattern.
